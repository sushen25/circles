# Voice

Lifted from the Wenna design manifesto §4. Content speaks the way the product
does: like a considerate friend who is good at organising, not like software
and not like a brand.

**It is:** plain, warm, specific, unhurried, slightly Australian in cadence
without performing it.

**It is not:** chirpy, urgent, clever, apologetic, corporate.

## Rules

- Say the thing. "Send my times", not "Submit availability".
- Name people, not counts, where a count would sting. "Doesn't work for Nina",
  never "1 unavailable".
- No exclamation marks. None. Not even in captions.
- No manufactured urgency. No "last chance", no countdowns.
- Never blame the user or the group. The subject of a failure sentence is the
  situation: "Not enough people were free this time."
- Numbers are paired with meaning. "5 of 6 can make it", never a bare "5".
- Emoji are not part of the voice. In a fictional chat a character may use
  one; Wenna's own captions never do.
- Second person for the reader, first person for their own data.
- Never position against the group chat. The group chat is where friends
  live; Wenna is the one link you paste into it.
- Never claim to cure loneliness, fix friendships, or make anyone a better
  friend.

## AI tells the reviewer fails on

- "In today's fast-paced world", "Let's be honest", "Here's the thing".
- Tidy triads ("faster, easier, better").
- Rhetorical question then one-word answer ("The result? Chaos.").
- Em-dash cascades. One per paragraph at most.
- Hashtag walls. Bluesky and Threads: none. Instagram: at most five, on
  their own line at the end of the caption (the publisher adds them from
  `hashtags:`). LinkedIn: none.
- Closing CTAs that beg ("Follow for more", "Link in bio" as the whole last
  line).
- Any sentence that could appear unchanged in a work scheduling tool's
  marketing.

## Skit characters

Fictional group chats use first names that read as a real Melbourne friend
group: mixed, ordinary, no surnames. Default cast, rotate freely: Priya, Tom,
Nina, Marcus, Jess, Dev, Ash, Harriet, Sam, Leila. Nobody is the villain; the
situation is.

## Worked examples

Good hook: "13 messages. No date."
Bad hook: "Tired of your group chat NEVER making plans? 😩"

Good last slide: "One link in the chat. Everyone marks the times they'd
actually be up for. Someone locks it in." + "wenna.app"
(Write it plain everywhere. The publisher adds the `?from=` tag; a caption
with `?from=` typed in fails `npm run check`.)
Bad last slide: "Download Wenna today and never miss a hangout again!"

Good Bluesky caption: "Our book club has been 'next month' since April. Made
a small thing so one person doesn't have to chase everyone. Still a work in
progress."
Bad Bluesky caption: "Introducing Wenna 🚀 The easiest way to plan with
friends. Try it free → wenna.app #startup #buildinpublic"
